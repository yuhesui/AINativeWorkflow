# Two-Page Education Submission — Content Outline

## Working title

**AI Native Workflow for Research: Formalizing the AI Workflow Researchers Already Use**

## 1. Concept

Researchers increasingly combine persistent chat for planning and interpretation, research agents for literature/evidence, and coding agents for implementation and experiments. This resource teaches how to turn that informal arrangement into an adaptable **AI-native research workflow**: durable intent and priority, hierarchical Plan/Phase structure, one human-readable DIC per Phase, coding-agent orchestration, bounded parallel subagents, evidence-linked execution, and verification-triggered repair/replanning. The central design objective is to reduce human attention spent on routine coordination per unit of verified research progress while retaining human authority over scientific meaning and consequential decisions.

Hierarchical planning is an enabling mechanism rather than the novelty claim by itself. The resource shows how current AI settings already make this style of long-horizon planning/execution practical.

## 2. Level and prerequisites

Advanced undergraduate / early graduate. Learners should understand ordinary LLM chat, tool use, and basic dependency graphs. No prior multi-agent or verification background is required.

## 3. Learning objectives

Learners will identify the hidden coordination burden in an existing AI workflow; distinguish Primary Aim from constraints/supporting branches; map a research project into Plan/Phase/DIC structure; identify safe parallelism and evidence gates; distinguish accept/repair/replan/escalate; and adapt the workflow—or selected components—to their own research practice.

## 4. Linked recent papers

Use ReAct (ICLR 2023), Reflexion (NeurIPS 2023), LATS (ICML 2024), LLMCompiler (ICML 2024), SWE-agent (NeurIPS 2024), and Automated Design of Agentic Systems (ICLR 2025) to motivate acting, feedback, search/planning, dependency-aware parallel execution, agent-computer interfaces, and compound-agent design. The educational contribution is the practical synthesis into a researcher-adaptable workflow.

## 5. Teaching materials

A 15–20 minute modular slide deck with short-cut variants; one deterministic dependency/repair demo; a learner exercise mapping a research project into Primary Aim, milestones, parallel branches, evidence, and human gates; an instructor solution; a facilitator guide; a Quick Start `.ai-workflow` teaching subset; and an annotated reading map.

## 6. Scope and limitations

The resource does not claim that hierarchical planning is new, that the workflow universally saves time/tokens, or that human-attention efficiency is already empirically established. It teaches a formalized operating pattern researchers can adopt wholesale or use to strengthen an existing workflow.
